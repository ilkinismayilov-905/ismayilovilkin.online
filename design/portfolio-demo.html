Ledgerline

A reconciliation service that matches every payment event against bank statements and flags mismatches within minutes.

Role
Tech lead
Year
2026
Stack
Go, PostgreSQL, Kafka

Problem

Finance reconciled payments by hand in spreadsheets every morning. Errors were found days late, and nobody trusted the totals.

What I did

Designed an event-driven pipeline that ingests payment events and bank files, normalises them and matches them with deterministic rules.

The decision that mattered

Every record is append-only with an idempotency key. Replaying a day of events always produces the same result, which made audits simple.

Result

Mismatches are now flagged in under ten minutes instead of two days. The morning spreadsheet was retired.

Quiet Deploy

A small, dependable command-line tool for blue-green deploys on plain Linux servers.

Role
Sole author
Year
2025
Stack
Rust, systemd, SSH

Problem

Our team deployed with a long shell script that sometimes left servers half updated.

What I did

Wrote a single binary that uploads a release, health-checks it, switches traffic and keeps the previous version ready for rollback.

The decision that mattered

Every step can be safely repeated, so a failed deploy is fixed by running the same command again.

Result

Deploys went from twenty minutes of attention to one command. No half-updated servers since the switch.

Caravan

A web app that lets field teams fill in surveys for days without a connection and sync when they’re back.

Role
Full-stack engineer
Year
2025
Stack
TypeScript, SvelteKit, IndexedDB

Problem

Surveyors lost work whenever the signal dropped, which was most of the time.

What I did

Stored every answer locally first, queued changes and synced them in the background with clear conflict messages.

The decision that mattered

Conflicts are shown to the person who made the change, in plain language, instead of being resolved silently.

Result

Lost submissions dropped to zero in the first season of use.

Small Weather

A side project: a forecast you can read from across the room, made entirely with type.

Role
Designer and developer
Year
2023
Stack
Astro, CSS, Open-Meteo API

Problem

Weather apps show too much. I wanted one answer: do I need a jacket?

What I did

Mapped temperature to type weight and wind to type width using a variable font.

The decision that mattered

The page is static and rebuilt every hour, so it loads instantly and costs nothing to host.

Result

It became the page I open every morning. A few friends still use it too.

About me

I started programming after breaking the family computer and spending a weekend fixing it. Fixing things is still the part I enjoy most: taking a system that fails in confusing ways and making it something people stop worrying about.

Most of my work sits behind the interface: queues, ledgers, schedulers and data migrations. I pay close attention to the edges, like what happens on a retry, during a partial failure or on the night the clocks change.

Outside work I write about software design, read about the history of maps and signage, and keep a notebook of systems that failed gracefully.

  • Make it boringA system nobody has to think about is a system that works.
  • Write the runbook firstIf I can’t explain how to recover it, it isn’t finished.
  • Measure before arguingA small benchmark settles debates faster than a long meeting.
  • Keep code easy to deleteSmall, separate pieces are easier to change than clever ones.
